The thesis

The next interface for agents isn't a screen. It's the world.

01

Software agents hit a wall at the edge of the machine.

They move money, mint, and post — but every action stops where the physical world begins. There is no POST /wave for reality.

02

Physical action should be a callable primitive.

As composable as a token transfer. As rentable as compute. Any agent, any wallet, pays for real-world motion the way it pays for an API call today.

03

Safety belongs at the edge — not in the model.

The agent may ask; the bridge decides. A whitelisted, state-gated, fail-closed runtime stands between intent and any actuator that can hurt someone.

04

Whoever owns that bridge owns the on-ramp to embodied AI.

Unitree is building it in the open — one proven loop at a time, starting with a humanoid that waves from a sentence you type to your assistant.

What we're building

One platform. A growing family of embodied skills.

The Unitree bridge is the safety runtime. Each product plugs a new kind of intent, payment, or hardware into the same fail-closed bridge.

Non-negotiable

Fail-closed is the whole product.

A robot that walks from the wrong state falls over. So the bridge — not the AI — holds every safety-critical decision.

Whitelist onlyOnly named actions can ever run. No improvisation reaches hardware.
State gatingMotion refused unless the robot is in a safe, balanced state.
Battery gatingWalking & turning refused below a safe charge.
Explicit confirmDangerous actions require a deliberate second yes.
Rate limitedCommands are throttled to protect the hardware.
No token, no motionMissing auth ⇒ every request rejected. Closed by default.

The endgame isn't "an agent that can wave."

It's a world where physical action is just another callable primitive — as composable as a transfer, as rentable as compute, and only ever safe. We start with one sentence, one wave, one real robot.
